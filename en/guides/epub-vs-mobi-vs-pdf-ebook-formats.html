EPUB vs MOBI vs AZW3 vs FB2 vs PDF: which ebook format should you use?

Ebook sites often offer the same book in four or five formats. Here is what each one is, what it is good at, and which to pick when you read on an Android phone.

Updated November 15, 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer

Pick EPUB whenever it is offered. It is the open standard, it reflows to fit any screen and almost every reader supports it. Choose PDF only for fixed-layout documents, and CBZ for comics. MOBI, AZW3 and FB2 are fine if that is all you have.

The quick comparison

  • EPUB: the open standard for ebooks. Text reflows to the screen, with adjustable font size and a table of contents. The best default.
  • MOBI: an older format from the Mobipocket era, later used by Kindle. Reflowable, but mostly legacy now.
  • AZW3 (KF8): Amazon's newer Kindle format, closer to EPUB. Books bought from Amazon are usually protected with DRM.
  • FB2 (FictionBook): an XML format popular in Russian-language collections. Reflowable, often zipped as .fb2.zip.
  • PDF: a fixed-layout document. Pages look the same everywhere, which suits printed layouts but not small screens.
  • CBZ and CBR: comics, as archives of page images. CBZ uses ZIP; CBR uses RAR.
  • DOCX, ODT, TXT: documents rather than ebooks, but easy to read as books once converted.

Reflowable vs fixed layout

Formats such as EPUB, MOBI, AZW3 and FB2 are reflowable: the reader decides how to lay out the text, so you can enlarge it, change the font or switch to night mode and the page rearranges itself. PDF and comics are fixed layout: each page is a picture of the page. On a phone, a PDF designed for A4 paper often needs zooming and sideways scrolling, while the same book as an EPUB reads comfortably.

DRM and where files come from

DRM is a lock that ties a book to a store or an account. Ebooks sold by stores often carry it, as do library loans. Free sources such as Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks do not. A reader that has no account system cannot open DRM-protected books. See how to open EPUB files on Android for what an .acsm file is.

How each format works on a phone

Here is how Balda treats each one:

  • EPUB is read natively, with highlights, notes, bookmarks, search and read aloud.
  • FB2, MOBI, AZW3 (without DRM), DOCX and ODT are converted to EPUB when you import them, so they get the same features. Books bought from Amazon are rejected because of DRM.
  • PDF opens in its own viewer, with pinch to zoom up to 4×. Text search in PDFs needs Android 15 or later. There are no highlights or read aloud for PDFs.
  • TXT and MD are read as plain text, in paged or scrolling mode, with read aloud.
  • CBZ opens as a comic, one page per screen. CBR is not supported: see how to convert CBR to CBZ.

Which to download

  1. If there is an EPUB, take it.
  2. If not, an AZW3 or MOBI without DRM, or an FB2, are good alternatives. They convert cleanly.
  3. Use a PDF when the layout matters: a textbook with diagrams, a form, a scanned page.
  4. Use CBZ for comics and manga.

Frequently asked questions

Is MOBI dead?

It is mostly a legacy format. You will still find plenty of MOBI files, and a reader that can import them, like Balda, handles them without any trouble.

Why does a PDF look so small on my phone?

Because a PDF is a fixed layout, usually sized for paper. The text does not reflow, so you zoom in and scroll. If you can get the same book as an EPUB, it will read much better.

Does converting a book to EPUB lose anything?

For plain text and images, usually not. Complex layouts, such as multi-column pages or floating text boxes, may not survive conversion from documents like DOCX.
