Where to download free ebooks legally

Thousands of classics are free to download and keep, with no catch. Here are the sources worth knowing, and what to do with the files once you have them.

Updated November 15, 2026 · 5 min read

Short answer

Start with Project Gutenberg and Standard Ebooks for public-domain classics in EPUB, then try Internet Archive and Wikisource. Download an EPUB, tap it on your phone and open it in a reader such as Balda.

Good places to look

  • Project Gutenberg: the oldest free ebook library, with more than 70,000 books, mostly public-domain classics in many languages. Each book comes in several formats, including EPUB, Kindle and plain text.
  • Standard Ebooks: public-domain books that volunteers have carefully proofread, typeset and given a consistent style. If a title is available here, this is usually the best-looking edition.
  • Internet Archive: an enormous digital library. Look for items marked as public domain or with a download option for EPUB or PDF. Books you can only borrow are protected and will not open in a plain reader.
  • Wikisource: a library of source texts. Many works can be exported as an EPUB from the page's tools menu.

You can also check whether the author or publisher gives away a book on their own site: many writers offer a free EPUB of an older title or a sample of a new one.

How to download and read a book

  1. Find the book on one of the sites above using your phone's browser.
  2. Choose an EPUB download. Pick the version with images if you want illustrations.
  3. When the download finishes, tap it in the notification or open it from Downloads, and choose Balda under “Open with”. If you cannot find it, see where your downloaded ebooks are.

Not sure which reader to use? Balda is free, needs no account and works offline. On first launch, a new library even comes with a couple of public-domain classics from Project Gutenberg in your language, so you can try the reader before adding anything.

A word about public domain

A book is in the public domain when its copyright has expired, and that depends on the country. Project Gutenberg's collection is public domain in the United States, and a few titles may still be protected elsewhere. If you are unsure, check the rules where you live.

What to avoid

  • Sites offering recent bestsellers for free. They are almost always infringing copyright, and the files can carry malware.
  • Files that ask you to install something. A book is an EPUB, PDF, MOBI or similar file. Anything that comes as an app or an installer is not a book.
  • Library loans with DRM. Apps from your local library lend books you read inside that app. They are a great free option, but those books cannot be opened in a separate reader.

Frequently asked questions

Which format should I download?

EPUB, whenever it is offered. It is the standard ebook format and works in virtually every reader. Balda also opens FB2, MOBI and AZW3 without DRM, PDF and plain text. See how to open EPUB files on Android.

Can I read free ebooks in other languages?

Yes. Project Gutenberg and Wikisource have books in many languages, and Balda is available in English, Spanish, French, German, Italian and Brazilian Portuguese.

Can my phone read the book aloud?

Yes. Balda reads EPUB, MOBI, AZW3, FB2 and TXT books aloud with your phone's own voice. See how to have your phone read a book aloud.
